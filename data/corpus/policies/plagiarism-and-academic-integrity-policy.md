# Plagiarism and Academic Integrity Policy

This policy defines plagiarism and sets the similarity thresholds and penalties for assignments, reports and projects submitted by B.Tech students at Sutlej Valley University (SVU) in 2026-27. Cheating in examinations is covered separately in the Student Conduct and Unfair Means Regulations.

## 1. What is plagiarism

Plagiarism means presenting the words, ideas, code, data or figures of another person or source as one's own without proper acknowledgement. It includes copying text from the internet or a book, copying a classmate's assignment or program, buying work from others, and using text generated by an AI tool without declaring it.

Plagiarism also covers self-plagiarism, which means submitting the same work for two different courses without permission. Reasonable collaboration is allowed only when the instructor permits it in writing in the assignment instructions. Every student is expected to read this policy at the start of each academic year.

## 2. Similarity checking process

All written assignments, project reports, lab reports and theses must be submitted through the student portal. The portal runs every submission through the licensed similarity-checking software of the university. The similarity report is available to the course instructor within 2 working days of the submission deadline.

Students can see their own similarity percentage on the portal. A student may submit a draft once before the final deadline to check the similarity, but only the final submission is evaluated. Program source code is compared using a separate code similarity tool, and code in the lab assignments of Data Structures and DBMS courses is also checked this way.

## 3. Similarity thresholds and penalties

The overall similarity percentage determines the action taken for a course assignment or report.

| Similarity percentage | Action |
|---|---|
| Up to 15% | Acceptable, no action |
| More than 15% and up to 30% | Rewrite and resubmit within 7 days, with marks capped at 70% of the maximum |
| More than 30% and up to 50% | Zero marks in that assessment |
| More than 50% | Grade F in the course and referral to the Academic Integrity Committee |

The instructor may use judgement to reduce the penalty if the similarity is due to common technical text. The final decision on any penalty rests with the Academic Integrity Committee.

## 4. Stricter limits for project reports and theses

The major project report (Project Phase I and Phase II) and any research paper written as a part of the programme are held to a stricter limit. The overall similarity must be at most 10%, and the similarity with any single source must not exceed 5%.

A project report with a similarity above 10% must be revised and resubmitted before the guide can sign the approval for the viva. A report with a similarity above 25% is rejected, and the student has to repeat the project phase in the next semester. The final project report cannot be submitted to the examination office without a similarity certificate signed by the guide.

## 5. Content excluded from the similarity count

The following content is not counted when the percentage is evaluated: the bibliography and reference list, properly quoted text of up to 40 words from one source, the cover page, declaration and table of contents, and standard code from the course template given by the instructor.

Any single match of less than 1% from one source is also ignored. Quoted material must be put in quotation marks and cited. Paraphrasing without a citation is still plagiarism even if it does not cause a high similarity percentage, and the instructor may refer such cases to the Committee.

## 6. Use of AI tools

Generative AI tools may be used for an assignment only if the course instructor permits it in the assignment instructions. When AI use is permitted, the student must add an AI use statement at the end of the work, naming the tool and describing how it was used.

AI-generated text or code that is not declared is treated as plagiarism and falls under the penalty for more than 30% similarity, which is zero marks in the assessment. The student remains fully responsible for the correctness of any AI-assisted work, and may be asked to explain it in a viva.

## 7. Repeat offences

The penalties become heavier for repeated plagiarism. A second proven case in the programme leads to grade F in the course and suspension from classes for one semester. A third proven case leads to a recommendation of expulsion to the Vice-Chancellor.

Cases of plagiarism in the major project, or the sale of assignments to other students, are always referred straight to the Academic Integrity Committee, whatever the similarity percentage. The record of the offence is kept in the student's confidential file, and is not shown on the grade card or the transcript.

## 8. Hearing and appeal

When a case is referred to the Academic Integrity Committee, the student is informed by email and has 5 working days to submit a written explanation. The Committee, chaired by the Dean Academics, holds a hearing and announces its decision within 10 working days.

A student who disagrees with the decision can appeal in writing to the Vice-Chancellor within 7 days of receiving the order. During the process, the grade of the course concerned remains on hold. Questions about the policy can be sent to integrity@svu.example.
