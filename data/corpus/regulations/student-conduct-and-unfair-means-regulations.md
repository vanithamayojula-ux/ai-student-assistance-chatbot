# Student Conduct and Unfair Means Regulations

This document lists the rules of conduct that every student of Sutlej Valley University (SVU) must follow on campus, in hostels and in examinations, along with the penalties for breaking them. It applies in the academic year 2026-27.

## 1. General code of conduct

Every student must carry the SVU identity card at all times on campus and show it on request to security staff or faculty. Students are expected to behave respectfully towards fellow students, faculty and staff, and to protect university property. Damage to property must be paid for by the student responsible, and a fine of up to Rs. 5,000 may be added.

Smoking, vaping and the use of alcohol or any narcotic substance are prohibited on campus and in hostels. A fine of Rs. 2,000 is charged for a first violation of the smoking rule. Use or possession of narcotics leads to suspension and a report to the police. Formal or smart-casual dress is required in classrooms and laboratories.

## 2. Anti-ragging rules

Ragging in any form is strictly prohibited at SVU. Ragging means any act that causes physical or mental harm or embarrassment to a student, including forced introductions, teasing, financial demands or forced actions. Every student and parent signs an anti-ragging undertaking at admission.

The Anti-Ragging Committee, chaired by the Dean Student Affairs, handles complaints. Complaints can be sent to antiragging@svu.example or made by calling the 24-hour helpline 1800-555-0147. Penalties include suspension from classes, removal from the hostel, a fine, expulsion from the university and an FIR with the police in serious cases. Complaints can be made anonymously.

## 3. Conduct inside the examination hall

Students must reach the examination hall at least 30 minutes before the start of the paper and must carry their hall ticket and SVU identity card. Students without a hall ticket are not allowed to enter, but they may collect a duplicate from the Controller of Examinations office for Rs. 200.

Mobile phones, smart watches, earphones and any electronic device are not allowed inside the hall, even if switched off. Only a non-programmable scientific calculator is permitted, and only when the question paper states so. Students must not talk to each other, exchange materials or leave the hall without permission from the invigilator.

## 4. What counts as unfair means

The following acts are considered unfair means in examinations, tests and other assessments:

- Possessing or using notes, books, chits, writing on the body or any electronic device in the hall.
- Copying from another student or allowing another student to copy.
- Impersonation, which means appearing for another student or allowing another person to appear in one's place.
- Tampering with answer scripts, changing marks or removing pages.
- Using proxy attendance, forging signatures or submitting forged medical certificates.
- Passing off copied work as one's own in assignments, which is handled under the Plagiarism and Academic Integrity Policy.

## 5. Penalties for unfair means in examinations

The penalty depends on the type of offence and on whether it is a first or repeated offence.

| Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|
| First offence of possessing unauthorised material or copying | Zero marks in that course and grade F |
| Second offence of the same kind, or copying in groups | Cancellation of all courses of that semester's examination |
| Impersonation or tampering with answer scripts | Cancellation of the semester and debarment for the next semester |
| Any third offence, or a very serious offence | Expulsion from the university |

The penalty does not remove the student's right to appeal, and the disciplinary record is kept confidential in the student's file.

## 6. Procedure and appeal

When an invigilator reports unfair means, the answer script and the material seized are sent to the Unfair Means Committee, which is headed by the Controller of Examinations. The student is given a written notice and 5 working days to submit a reply, and then appears before the Committee.

The Committee announces its decision within 10 working days of the hearing. A student who disagrees can appeal in writing to the Vice-Chancellor within 15 days of receiving the order. The decision of the Vice-Chancellor is final. During the inquiry, the result of the course concerned is kept on hold.

## 7. Misuse of IT and social media

Students must follow the IT Acceptable Use Policy while using the campus network, the student portal and the email account. Sharing the portal password, hacking, downloading pirated software, accessing adult content and sending abusive or threatening messages are prohibited.

Posting content that defames the university, faculty or fellow students on social media is considered misconduct. The first violation leads to a written warning and loss of Wi-Fi access for 15 days. Repeated violations are referred to the Disciplinary Committee, which may suspend the student for up to one semester.

## 8. Disciplinary Committee and minor penalties

Minor violations of the code of conduct, such as not carrying the ID card, damaging property or leaving classes without permission, are dealt with by the Dean Student Affairs. Penalties include a written warning, a fine of Rs. 500 to Rs. 5,000, community service on campus for up to 20 hours, and parent notification.

More serious cases go to the Disciplinary Committee, which has the Dean Student Affairs as chair and one faculty member from the student's department. The committee can suspend a student for up to one semester. The student has the right to be heard before any penalty is decided.
