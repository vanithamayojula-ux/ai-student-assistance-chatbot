# Discrete Mathematics Syllabus (AICTE Model Curriculum, B.Tech CSE)

This is the Discrete Mathematics syllabus from the AICTE Model Curriculum for the UG degree in Computer Science and Engineering (revised 2022), published by AICTE, the All India Council for Technical Education. It is a model syllabus that universities adapt. It is not LPU's own syllabus: LPU does not publish its course syllabi on its public website, so LPU's course code, credits and units may differ. In LPU's published sample B.Tech CSE curriculum, Discrete Mathematics is a Year 2, Autumn term course.

## Topics at a glance

**Q:** What topics are covered in the Discrete Mathematics syllabus?
**A:** The AICTE Discrete Mathematics syllabus covers six essential modules: sets, relations and functions (including countability and Cantor's diagonal argument), proof strategies (contradiction, contraposition, induction), modular arithmetic (Euclid's GCD algorithm, Chinese Remainder Theorem, RSA), combinatorics (pigeonhole principle, generating functions, recurrences), graph theory (Eulerian/Hamiltonian walks, coloring, planarity, matching), and propositional and first-order logic.

## Module 1: Sets, relations and functions (about 1.5 weeks)

- Operations and laws of sets, Cartesian products, binary relations, partial ordering relations and equivalence relations.
- Image of a set, sum and product of functions, bijective functions, inverse and composite functions.
- Size of a set, finite and infinite sets, countable and uncountable sets, Cantor's diagonal argument and the power set theorem.
- Computer science applications: uncountability of reals, uncomputability via diagonalization, and relational databases.

## Module 2: Proof strategies (1.5 weeks)

- Proof methods and strategies: forward proof, proof by contradiction, proof by contraposition, necessity and sufficiency, case analysis and mathematical induction.
- Examples such as irrationality of the square root of 2 and infinitude of primes.

## Module 3: Modular arithmetic (1.5 weeks)

- Extended Euclid's Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) algorithm and the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
- Modular arithmetic, coprimality (Euler's totient function) and the Chinese Remainder Theorem.
- Computer science applications: Diffie-Hellman key exchange and the RSA cryptosystem.

## Module 4: Combinatorics (1.5 weeks)

- Permutations and combinations, inclusion-exclusion principle and the pigeonhole principle.
- Generating functions and recurrence relations.
- Applications: counting binary trees, matched parentheses and matrix chain multiplications.

## Module 5: Graphs (1.5 weeks)

- Connected components, paths, cycles and trees.
- Hamiltonian and Eulerian walks, graph coloring, planarity and matching.
- Computer science applications: shortest-distance paths, minimum spanning trees, prefix codes and the graph isomorphism problem.

## Module 6: Logic (1.5 weeks)

- Propositional logic and first-order logic: translating natural-language sentences and predicates into formal logic.
- Semantics of first-order logic: interpretations and formula evaluation; optional topics on semantic entailment, validity, satisfiability, proof systems (natural deduction or analytical tableau), consistency and completeness.
- Computer science applications: SAT solvers, relational calculus, specification languages such as Z, and Hoare logic for program verification.

## Desirable modules (optional)

- Algebra (1.5 weeks): groups, permutation groups, cosets, normal subgroups, rings, fields, finite fields, Fermat's little theorem, homomorphisms and isomorphisms, with applications to RSA and Reed-Solomon error-correcting codes.
- Discrete probability (1.5 weeks): discrete sample spaces, probability distributions, random variables, expectation, variance, Bernoulli trials, conditional probability, independence and Bayes' theorem, applied to randomized algorithms and heuristics.
