# Theory of Computation Syllabus (AICTE Model Curriculum, B.Tech CSE)

This is the Theory of Computation (automata theory) syllabus from the AICTE Model Curriculum for the UG degree in Computer Science and Engineering (revised 2022), published by AICTE, the All India Council for Technical Education. It is a model syllabus that universities adapt. It is not LPU's own syllabus: LPU does not publish its course syllabi on its public website. LPU's sample B.Tech CSE curriculum has a related course called Formal Languages and Automation Theory in Year 3, Spring term; its code, credits and units may differ from this model.

## Topics at a glance

**Q:** What topics are covered in the Theory of Computation syllabus?
**A:** The AICTE Theory of Computation syllabus covers three modules: finite automata and regular languages (DFA, NFA, regular expressions, pumping lemma), context-free grammars and pushdown automata, and Turing machines, decidability and complexity (P, NP and EXP).

Topics are marked in the AICTE syllabus as compulsory, suggested (optional) or advanced.

## Module 1: Finite automata and regular languages (4 to 5 weeks)

- Why automata theory; alphabets, formal languages and problems.
- Deterministic finite automata (DFA) and arguing that a DFA is correct.
- Closure properties of regular languages and the product construction.
- Limits of finite automata: non-regular languages and the pumping lemma.
- Non-deterministic finite automata (NFA), the subset construction, and equivalence with DFAs.
- Regular expressions and their equivalence with regular languages.
- DFA minimization and its algorithm.
- Suggested: Myhill-Nerode relations. Advanced: two-way DFAs (2DFA).

## Module 2: Grammars, context-free languages and pushdown automata (4 to 5 weeks)

- Grammars and their motivation from language theory.
- Context-free grammars (CFG), closure properties, and Chomsky Normal Form.
- Pushdown automata (PDA), acceptance by empty stack vs final state, and equivalence of PDAs and CFGs.
- Non-context-free languages and the pumping lemma for context-free languages.
- Deterministic context-free languages and deterministic PDAs.
- Suggested: the CYK parsing algorithm. Advanced: Ogden's lemma.

## Module 3: Turing machines and computability (4 to 5 weeks)

- Turing machines, equivalent models, and the Church-Turing hypothesis.
- Decidable and Turing-recognizable (recursive and recursively enumerable) languages, and their closure properties.
- Undecidability by diagonalization, and reductions to prove undecidability.
- Resource-bounded Turing machines and an introduction to complexity: the classes P, NP and EXP.
- Suggested: Post's correspondence problem; polynomial-time reductions, NP-completeness and the Cook-Levin theorem without proof.
- Advanced: Rice's theorem; space-bounded computation and PSPACE.

## Textbooks and references

Textbooks: Theory of Computation (Patel, Khanna, 2020); Introduction to the Theory of Computation (Sipser, 3rd edition); Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages and Computation (Hopcroft, Motwani and Ullman, 3rd edition).

References: Automata and Computability (Kozen); Elements of the Theory of Computation (Lewis and Papadimitriou, 2nd edition).
