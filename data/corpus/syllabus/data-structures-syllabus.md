# Data Structures (CS201) Syllabus

This is the official syllabus of the course Data Structures (CS201), taught to B.Tech CSE students in Semester 3 at Sutlej Valley University (SVU). It lists the course details, units, laboratory work, books and the marks scheme.

## 1. Course details of Data Structures CS201

Data Structures has the course code CS201 and is taught in Semester 3. It is a 3-0-2 course worth 4 credits: three lecture hours and two practical hours per week, with no separate tutorial. The course runs for 14 teaching weeks and has 42 lecture hours in total.

The course coordinator is Dr. Harpreet Kaur Sandhu, whose office is Block C, Room C-214, and who can be reached at harpreet.sandhu@svu.example. Practical sessions take place in the Programming Lab, Block C, Room C-Lab 1. The course is offered by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering and is a core course for all CSE students.

## 2. Prerequisites for Data Structures

Students must have completed Programming for Problem Solving (CS101) and Object Oriented Programming using Java (CS102) before registering for Data Structures. Both courses are taught in the first year of the B.Tech CSE programme.

Discrete Mathematics (MA201) is a co-requisite, which means it is taken in the same semester, because the unit on trees and graphs uses basic ideas of sets, relations and counting. Students who have a backlog in CS101 or CS102 are advised to clear it first, but they may register for CS201 if they meet the promotion rule of the Academic Progression Regulations. Laboratory work uses C++, which is introduced in the first two lab sessions.

## 3. Learning outcomes of CS201

After completing Data Structures, a student should be able to do the following:

- CO1: Implement arrays, linked lists, stacks and queues and explain their time complexity.
- CO2: Apply stacks and queues to problems such as expression evaluation and scheduling.
- CO3: Build and traverse binary trees, binary search trees and AVL trees.
- CO4: Represent graphs and use BFS, DFS and shortest-path algorithms.
- CO5: Choose a suitable sorting, searching or hashing method for a given problem and justify the choice with complexity analysis.

These outcomes are assessed through the mid-term, quizzes, laboratory work and the end-term examination.

## 4. Units I to III of Data Structures

**Unit I: Introduction and linear lists (8 hours).** Abstract data types, algorithm analysis, Big-O notation, arrays, singly, doubly and circular linked lists.

**Unit II: Stacks and queues (8 hours).** Stack operations, infix to postfix conversion, expression evaluation, recursion, simple queue, circular queue, deque and priority queue.

**Unit III: Trees (9 hours).** Binary trees, tree traversals, binary search trees, AVL trees with rotations, heaps and heap sort, and an introduction to B-trees.

The mid-term examination covers Units I and II and the first part of Unit III, as announced by the course coordinator at least two weeks before the exam.

## 5. Units IV and V of Data Structures

**Unit IV: Graphs (9 hours).** Graph terminology, adjacency matrix and adjacency list, breadth-first search, depth-first search, connected components, topological sort, minimum spanning trees using Prim and Kruskal algorithms, and Dijkstra shortest path.

**Unit V: Searching, sorting and hashing (8 hours).** Linear and binary search, bubble, insertion, selection, merge and quick sort, radix sort, hash functions, collision resolution by chaining and open addressing, and comparison of sorting algorithms.

The five units total 42 lecture hours: 8, 8, 9, 9 and 8 hours respectively. The end-term examination covers the whole syllabus with extra weight on Units III to V.

## 6. Laboratory component of CS201

The laboratory part of Data Structures consists of 12 experiments performed in C++ across 14 weekly sessions of 2 hours each. Experiments include array operations, linked list implementation, stack-based expression evaluation, queue simulation, binary search tree operations, AVL tree insertion, graph traversal, shortest path, sorting comparison and a hashing program.

Each student keeps a lab record, which is signed after every session. The last two sessions are reserved for a viva and a practical test. Attendance in lab sessions counts towards the overall 75% attendance requirement of the course, and a missed lab session can be made up only with permission from the course coordinator within one week.

## 7. Textbooks and references for Data Structures

Prescribed textbooks:

1. Ellis Horowitz, Sartaj Sahni and Susan Anderson-Freed, Fundamentals of Data Structures in C, 2nd edition, Universities Press.
2. Mark Allen Weiss, Data Structures and Algorithm Analysis in C++, 4th edition, Pearson.

Reference books:

1. Thomas H. Cormen, Charles Leiserson, Ronald Rivest and Clifford Stein, Introduction to Algorithms, 3rd edition, MIT Press.
2. Seymour Lipschutz, Data Structures with C, Schaum's Outline Series, McGraw Hill.

Copies of all four books are available in the Central Library, Block A, second floor. A scanned set of lecture slides is posted on the student portal each week by the course coordinator.

## 8. Evaluation scheme of Data Structures

The total marks for Data Structures are 100, divided as shown below. The same scheme applies to every student in every section.

| Component | Marks |
|---|---|
| Mid-term examination | 20 |
| Quizzes (best 2 of 3) | 10 |
| Laboratory work (record, performance, viva) | 20 |
| End-term examination | 50 |
| Total | 100 |

To pass, a student needs at least 40 marks out of 100 overall and at least 20 marks out of 50 in the end-term examination. The mid-term is 90 minutes long and the end-term is 3 hours long. Grades are awarded on the university grade table in the Examination and Grading Regulations.
