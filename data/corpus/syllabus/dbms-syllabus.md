# Database Management Systems (CS211) Syllabus

This is the official syllabus of Database Management Systems (CS211), a core course for B.Tech CSE students in Semester 4 at Sutlej Valley University (SVU). It covers the course details, units, laboratory, books and the marks scheme.

## 1. Course details of Database Management Systems CS211

Database Management Systems (DBMS) has the course code CS211 and is taught in Semester 4. It is a 3-0-2 course carrying 4 credits, with three lecture hours and two practical hours per week over 14 teaching weeks, giving 42 lecture hours.

The course coordinator is Dr. Manpreet Singh Gill, Block C, Room C-221, email manpreet.gill@svu.example. Practical sessions are held in the Database Lab, Block C, Room C-305. The course is offered by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering. Students work with the PostgreSQL database system during the labs, and installation instructions are posted on the student portal in the first week of the semester.

## 2. Prerequisites for DBMS

Students must have completed Data Structures (CS201) and Discrete Mathematics (MA201) before registering for Database Management Systems. Data Structures provides the background on indexing structures such as trees and hashing, and Discrete Mathematics provides the basics of sets, relations and logic that underlie the relational model.

Knowledge of any programming language is helpful for the embedded SQL and mini-project parts of the course. Students who did not clear CS201 may register for CS211 only if they satisfy the promotion rule of the Academic Progression Regulations and have written permission from the course coordinator.

## 3. Learning outcomes of CS211

On completing the course, a student should be able to:

- CO1: Design an entity-relationship (ER) diagram and convert it to relational tables.
- CO2: Write complex SQL queries including joins, subqueries, views and aggregate functions.
- CO3: Normalize a relation up to Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) using functional dependencies.
- CO4: Explain transactions, ACID properties, concurrency control and recovery.
- CO5: Describe storage, indexing and query processing, and compare relational databases with NoSQL systems.

The outcomes are measured through assignments, the laboratory, the mid-term and the end-term examination.

## 4. Units I to III of DBMS

**Unit I: Introduction and data models (8 hours).** Database system architecture, data independence, ER model, extended ER, relational model, keys and relational algebra.

**Unit II: SQL (9 hours).** Data definition and manipulation, joins, nested queries, aggregate functions, views, triggers, stored procedures and embedded SQL.

**Unit III: Database design and normalization (8 hours).** Functional dependencies, closure, normal forms 1NF, 2NF, 3NF and BCNF, lossless decomposition and dependency preservation, and multivalued dependencies.

Unit II is the heaviest unit for the laboratory, because most lab experiments are based on SQL. The mid-term examination covers Units I and II.

## 5. Units IV and V of DBMS

**Unit IV: Transaction management (9 hours).** Transaction concept, ACID properties, schedules, serializability, two-phase locking, timestamp ordering, deadlock handling, log-based recovery and checkpoints.

**Unit V: Storage, indexing and NoSQL (8 hours).** File organization, B+ tree indexes, hashing, query processing and optimization basics, and an introduction to NoSQL databases covering key-value, document and graph stores.

The five units add up to 42 lecture hours: 8, 9, 8, 9 and 8 hours respectively. The end-term examination covers all five units, and at least one question in each section of the paper is drawn from Units IV and V.

## 6. Laboratory and mini-project of CS211

The DBMS laboratory has 12 weekly experiments on PostgreSQL covering DDL and DML commands, joins, subqueries, views, indexes, triggers, stored procedures and transactions. After the 10th week, each student completes a small database application as a mini-project, individually or in pairs.

The lab carries 20 marks: 10 marks for continuous evaluation of lab records and weekly performance, and 10 marks for a practical examination held in the last week of the semester. Lab sessions are for 2 hours per week, and a missed session counts as absence under the 75% attendance requirement of the Attendance Regulations.

## 7. Textbooks and references for DBMS

Prescribed textbooks:

1. Abraham Silberschatz, Henry F. Korth and S. Sudarshan, Database System Concepts, 7th edition, McGraw Hill.
2. Ramez Elmasri and Shamkant B. Navathe, Fundamentals of Database Systems, 7th edition, Pearson.

Reference books:

1. Raghu Ramakrishnan and Johannes Gehrke, Database Management Systems, 3rd edition, McGraw Hill.
2. Regina Obe and Leo Hsu, PostgreSQL: Up and Running, O'Reilly.

The Central Library, Block A, holds 10 copies of the first textbook. Previous year question papers for CS211 are available on the student portal under the Library tab.

## 8. Evaluation scheme of Database Management Systems

The total marks for Database Management Systems are 100, divided as follows.

| Component | Marks |
|---|---|
| Mid-term examination | 20 |
| Assignments (2 assignments) | 10 |
| Laboratory continuous evaluation | 10 |
| Laboratory practical examination | 10 |
| End-term examination | 50 |
| Total | 100 |

A student needs at least 40 marks out of 100 overall and at least 20 marks out of 50 in the end-term examination to pass. Both assignments are submitted through the student portal and are checked for similarity under the Plagiarism and Academic Integrity Policy. The mid-term is of 90 minutes and the end-term is of 3 hours.
