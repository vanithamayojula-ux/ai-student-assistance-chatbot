# Machine Learning (CS301) Syllabus

This is the official syllabus of Machine Learning (CS301), a core course for B.Tech CSE students in Semester 5 at Sutlej Valley University (SVU). It lists the course details, prerequisites, units, practical work, books and the marks scheme.

## 1. Course details of Machine Learning CS301

Machine Learning has the course code CS301 and is taught in Semester 5. It is a 3-0-2 course carrying 4 credits: three lecture hours and two practical hours per week over 14 teaching weeks, giving 42 lecture hours.

The course coordinator is Dr. Simran Arora, Block C, Room C-231, email simran.arora@svu.example. Practical sessions take place in the AI Lab, Block C, Room C-310, which has 40 workstations with GPUs. The course is offered by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering and counts as a core course. Students use Python with the scikit-learn library for labs, and the first lab session is a revision of NumPy and pandas.

## 2. Prerequisites for Machine Learning

Three prerequisites are required for Machine Learning: Probability and Statistics (MA202), Data Structures (CS201) and the Python for Data Science Lab (CS215). Probability and Statistics supplies the background for Bayesian methods and model evaluation. CS215 provides the Python programming skills that the lab depends on.

Linear algebra from Engineering Mathematics I (MA101) is assumed. A student who has a pending backlog in MA202 or CS215 must clear it or obtain written permission from the course coordinator before registering for CS301.

## 3. Learning outcomes of CS301

On completing the course, a student should be able to:

- CO1: Formulate a real-world problem as a supervised or unsupervised learning task.
- CO2: Implement and compare regression and classification algorithms.
- CO3: Evaluate models using cross-validation, confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1 score and ROC curves.
- CO4: Apply clustering and dimensionality reduction to unlabelled data.
- CO5: Build a basic neural network and discuss ethical issues such as bias and fairness.

These outcomes are assessed through the mid-term, quizzes, laboratory, the course project and the end-term examination.

## 4. Units I to III of Machine Learning

**Unit I: Introduction and regression (8 hours).** Types of learning, bias and variance, linear regression, gradient descent, polynomial regression, regularization using ridge and lasso.

**Unit II: Classification (9 hours).** Logistic regression, k-nearest neighbours, naive Bayes, decision trees, support vector machines and kernels.

**Unit III: Ensemble methods and model evaluation (8 hours).** Bagging, random forests, boosting, gradient boosting, cross-validation, hyperparameter tuning, confusion matrix, ROC and AUC, and handling imbalanced data.

The mid-term examination covers Units I and II. Students should bring a non-programmable scientific calculator to the mid-term, as mobile phones are not allowed.

## 5. Units IV and V of Machine Learning

**Unit IV: Unsupervised learning (8 hours).** K-means clustering, hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN, Gaussian mixture models, principal component analysis (PCA) and t-SNE for visualization.

**Unit V: Neural networks and responsible AI (9 hours).** Perceptron, multilayer perceptron, backpropagation, activation functions, an introduction to convolutional networks, model deployment basics, and ethics covering data bias, fairness, privacy and explainability.

The five units total 42 lecture hours: 8, 9, 8, 8 and 9 hours respectively. Deep learning in depth is not part of this course and is covered in the programme electives of later semesters.

## 6. Laboratory and course project of CS301

The laboratory consists of 10 weekly experiments in Python using scikit-learn and a small amount of TensorFlow. The experiments cover data cleaning, linear regression, logistic regression, k-nearest neighbours, decision trees, support vector machines, random forests, clustering, PCA and a simple neural network.

In addition, each student team of up to 3 members completes a course project on a dataset of its choice. The project proposal is due in week 6, a progress review is held in week 10, and the final demonstration and report are due in week 14. Reports are checked for similarity under the Plagiarism and Academic Integrity Policy. Datasets from the Indian government open data portal are encouraged.

## 7. Textbooks and references for Machine Learning

Prescribed textbooks:

1. Aurelien Geron, Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras and TensorFlow, 3rd edition, O'Reilly.
2. Tom M. Mitchell, Machine Learning, McGraw Hill.

Reference books:

1. Christopher M. Bishop, Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning, Springer.
2. Trevor Hastie, Robert Tibshirani and Jerome Friedman, The Elements of Statistical Learning, 2nd edition, Springer.
3. Ethem Alpaydin, Introduction to Machine Learning, 4th edition, MIT Press.

The Central Library, Block A, keeps 8 copies of the first textbook, and the library also provides online access to the O'Reilly learning platform through the campus network.

## 8. Evaluation scheme of Machine Learning

The total marks for Machine Learning are 100, divided as follows.

| Component | Marks |
|---|---|
| Mid-term examination | 20 |
| Quizzes (2 quizzes) | 10 |
| Laboratory work | 10 |
| Course project | 10 |
| End-term examination | 50 |
| Total | 100 |

A student needs at least 40 marks out of 100 overall and at least 20 marks out of 50 in the end-term examination to pass. The course project is evaluated on the proposal, the final demonstration, the report and a short viva. The mid-term lasts 90 minutes and the end-term lasts 3 hours.
